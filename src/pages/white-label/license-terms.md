# White-label license terms

**Last updated:** November 14, 2026 · This is the plain-words version of the white-label section of our [Terms of Service](/terms), which is the binding text.

## 1. What the license is

We publish and maintain Capacitor Auth Manager under your name. We rename the package, publish it to your npm scope, host its docs on your domain with your name and logo, and keep it updated. You may offer that package to your own developers and clients.

## 2. The code and the name

The source of `capacitor-auth-manager` is public on GitHub under the MIT license, and the white-label license changes nothing about that. Anyone may use, copy and modify the code under MIT. What the white-label license adds is our managed work on your build and the right to use the Capacitor Auth Manager name and logo with it, which MIT does not grant.

## 3. Not exclusive

We may license the same package to other buyers. Because the code is already open, we don't sell it outright.

## 4. What it covers

The license covers the npm package group: the published package with its web code and its iOS and Android plugin code, which ships inside the npm package as it does upstream. Apps you build with it, and their store accounts, are outside the license.

## 5. Updates

Releases of `capacitor-auth-manager` are carried into your build for six months from the purchase date. After that, a renewal of 3, 6 or 12 months continues them. Without one, the versions already published under your name stay on npm and keep working. Current renewal prices are on the [updates page](/white-label/updates).

## 6. Custom work

Changes made only for you, such as enabling a provider ahead of the upstream release, are [quoted and paid separately](/white-label/custom-work).

## 7. Verification

Each license has a key starting with `CAPAUTH-WL-`. Your docs domain serves a file at `/.well-known/capauth-white-label-license.json`, and anyone can check the key or the domain on the [verify page](/verify-license).

## 8. Revocation

A license used outside these terms, or on a package name or domain it doesn't cover, may be revoked.

## 9. Your own costs

Costs you choose yourself, such as your npm organization, your domain or a paid service you add, are yours.

Payment terms are in the [Terms of Service](/terms).
